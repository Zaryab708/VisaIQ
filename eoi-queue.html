Common questions
EOI queue FAQ
What is the average waiting time for Australian PR? +
It varies significantly by occupation tier. Tier 1 Healthcare (nurses, GPs, allied health) averages 3–8 months at current invitation rates. Tier 2 Education averages 6–14 months. Tier 3 Engineering and Trades averages 6–18 months depending on state. Tier 4 ICT and Accounting have received zero federal 189 invitations since mid-2024 — the 491 regional pathway is now the main route. Your exact wait depends on your occupation, points score, and whether you pursue state nomination in parallel.
Does submitting my EOI early improve my chances? +
No — and lodging early can hurt you. DHA selects by points score, not submission date. If you lodge below the likely cutoff and later get invited, your Date of Effect resets to the invitation date — not your original submission date. Only lodge when your score is at or above the cutoff for your occupation.
What's the fastest legal way to move up the queue? +
Three fastest moves: NAATI CCL adds 5 points in ~90 days for ~$800 (check naati.com.au for eligible languages — Urdu, Hindi, Nepali, Tagalog, Mandarin and others). Superior English adds 10 points over Proficient (IELTS 8.0+ in all 4 bands). State nomination via SA or TAS 190 adds 5 effective points AND puts you in a separate, often faster queue — both run monthly rounds.
What is Tier 1, 2, 3 and 4? +
DHA classifies skilled occupations into tiers based on national shortage and invitation priority. Tier 1 (Healthcare) receives the lowest cutoffs and most consistent invitations — nurses have been invited in every recent round at 75 pts. Tier 2 (Education and Social) receives regular invitations and most states nominate these occupations. Tier 3 (Engineering and Trades) varies widely — some trades like electricians still receive 189 invitations; engineers primarily use state 190 routes. Tier 4 (ICT and Accounting) have received no federal 189 invitations since mid-2024 and rely almost entirely on state nomination or regional 491 pathways.
Can I be in both the 189 and 190 queues simultaneously? +
Yes. You can lodge separate EOIs for 189 and 190 simultaneously and can hold one state nomination at a time. The smart strategy: lodge your 189 EOI immediately if above the cutoff to secure your Date of Effect, and simultaneously submit your 190 Register of Interest to SA and TAS — both run monthly rounds and are the most active states in 2026. Whichever comes first, accept it.
